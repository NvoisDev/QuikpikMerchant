Yeah — I agree with your review overall. It’s thoughtful, and more importantly, it’s grounded in how your system actually behaves (not theoretical “perfect architecture” stuff).

But I’d tighten a few things so you don’t accidentally over-engineer or slow yourself down.

Where you’re spot on ✅
Strictly additive approach — this is exactly right for your codebase
You’ve got a lot of moving parts (fees, stock, Stripe, notifications). Touching existing flows = risk. Extending instead = safe.
Separate draft + approve flows
This is the cleanest mental model:
Draft = no side effects
Approve = full existing pipeline
Drafts excluded from analytics
This is critical and easy to mess up. Good catch.
Idempotency on approve
Especially with Stripe + retries — this saves you from weird duplicate behaviour.
Where I’d push back slightly ⚠️
1. The modal idea

You already clocked it — it will slow users down.

Your instinct to replace it with:

Primary: “Create Invoice” (existing flow)
Secondary: “Save as Draft”

👉 That’s the better UX. No debate.

Modal = friction for everyone
Second button = power for those who need it

2. Draft editing needs a hard rule

Right now it’s a bit vague, and this is where bugs creep in.

You need to explicitly enforce:

Draft editing is a pure data update — no stock logic, no Stripe, no audit side effects.

If you don’t lock that down, someone later will reuse “edit quote” logic and accidentally:

restore stock
recreate Stripe sessions
trigger notifications

That’s where things break.

3. Stock on approval (this is the big one)

You called it out — but this is not optional to define.

Right now:

Draft → no stock reserved
Approval → stock deducted

So what happens if stock is gone?

You must define:

Approval fails with a clear error if stock is insufficient.

Otherwise you’ll get:

negative stock bugs
inconsistent inventory
support headaches
4. The time estimate

Your revised thinking is right.

This is not a 2-day feature unless you go heavy on testing + polish.

Realistically:

Part	Time
Schema + backend paths	1.5–2.5 hrs
Approve endpoint	~45 mins
UI changes (button + tab)	~1 hr
Basic tests	~1–2 hrs

👉 Total: ~4–6 hours (clean execution)

The only thing that pushes it to a day+ is:

deep regression tests
PDF watermark
edge-case hardening
Final verdict

Yes — I agree with your spec.

But I’d summarise it like this:

“This is a well-designed feature with the right architecture. The only real risks are UX friction (modal), unclear draft editing rules, and undefined stock behavior at approval.”

Fix those 3 things, and this becomes a clean, low-risk, high-value feature.
